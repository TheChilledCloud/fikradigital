حكاية بيت الشام
Home ✦ Our Story
1998
Bab Tuma · Damascus

It began with a pot of jameed

In a kitchen no bigger than a bedroom, grandmother Khadija fed the whole alley. Her mansaf pot never emptied on Fridays — neighbours arrived with bread, children with empty plates, and strangers with questions in broken Arabic. “Sit,” she would say. “Eat first. Talk later.”

بدأت الحكاية بقدور الجَميد
2005
The Crossing

A suitcase of recipes, a pocket full of cardamom

When the family left Damascus, the recipes came too — written on the inside of a notebook cover, weighed in handfuls, not grams. Cardamom pods travelled in a coat pocket. The first dinner in Germany was served on a borrowed table: rice, lentils, and a promise.

غادرنا دمشق.. والوصفات معنا
2016
Düsseldorf · Herzogstraße

A new home. The same fire.

Ten years of cooking for friends, neighbours and anyone who smelled the garlic from the stairwell — until Düsseldorf asked for more. Beit Al-Sham opened with six tables, one charcoal grill and a queue that wrapped around the block. It has never stopped.

بيت جديد.. ونار واحدة
اليوم
Today & Tomorrow

Sixty-two recipes. One promise.

The second generation now runs the grill. The menu has grown, the dining room has grown, the family that eats here has grown. But the promise grandmother Khadija made in 1998 still stands: cook it slow, cook it honest, and never let anyone leave hungry.

Our Rituals · طقوسنا

Hospitality is a language

In Damascus, a guest is never asked what they want. The coffee appears. The tea follows. The table fills. These are the rituals we refuse to modernise.

01

The dallah, always ready

Cardamom coffee brewed in the brass pot, poured from a height, served with dates — before you even ask.

02

Bread, never bought

Saj and pita are pressed in-house, all day. Cold bread is the only sin we take seriously.

03

The feast is shared

Mansaf, maqluba, ouzi — platters that arrive in the middle of the table and leave empty together.

04

Nobody leaves hungry

It's not a saying. It's the house rule, written on the kitchen wall in Arabic.

What we stand for · مبادئنا

Four words, written on the wall

Family

Every guest is treated like family visiting for Friday lunch. That's not marketing — it's upbringing.

عائلة

Freshness

No freezers on the line. Vegetables arrive daily from the market; bread is pressed to order.

نضارة

Tradition

Recipes measured in handfuls, cooked in copper, finished with patience. Some things don't need improving.

تراث

Hospitality

You will be welcomed loudly, fed generously and sent off with tea you didn't order.
